Why Kyrgyzstan

Six reasons Indian students choose Kyrgyzstan

Not brochure slogans — the things that actually shape your six years as a medical student.

A medical school since 1939

The Kyrgyz State Medical Academy opened in 1939 and sent out its first doctors in 1943. In 2026 the government named it the lead state institution for all medical training in the country.

Fees set with a floor, not a ceiling

The government fixes a minimum tuition of US$3,000 a year for foreign medical students and has said fees will not rise. Most families can plan all six years with confidence.

A cleaner system from 2026

Every medical university now needs state accreditation, and weak ones have lost the right to admit students. One official portal lists each university’s programmes, fees and language.

Just 30 minutes ahead of India

Your morning lecture starts as your parents finish breakfast. Calls home fit easily into your day, and a one-stop flight brings you home for the holidays.

A green city under the mountains

Bishkek sits at the foot of the snow-capped Ala-Too range, with tree-lined avenues and parks. Lake Issyk-Kul, one of the world’s largest mountain lakes, is a weekend trip away.

Thousands of Indian students

Kyrgyzstan is one of the most popular MBBS destinations for Indian families, so Indian food, festivals and seniors from your state are close at hand in Bishkek and Osh.

How the course works

From Class 12 to doctor: the Kyrgyz route

Kyrgyzstan offers a direct six-year MBBS, called General Medicine. There is no pre-medical degree. Here is how the years add up.

  1. 1
    Years 1–2

    Pre-clinical sciences

    Anatomy, physiology and biochemistry, plus Russian classes so you can talk to patients from your third year.

  2. 2
    Years 3–5

    Clinical years

    Ward rotations in the university’s own clinics and city hospitals across medicine, surgery, paediatrics and more.

  3. 3
    Year 6 · 12 months

    Internship

    Supervised clinical practice. NMC requires this year at the same university you studied in.

    5-year tracks don’t qualify
  4. 4
    Back in India

    FMGE / NExT

    Clear the licensing exam, complete any internship NMC asks of you, and register to practise.

In all, plan for six years abroad. Some universities sell a shorter five-year track with the internship “done in India” — NMC will not accept it. We only offer the full six-year route.

The 2026 rules · as of Oct 2026

Kyrgyzstan changed the rules in 2026 — here’s what it means for you

This year Kyrgyzstan put every medical university through state accreditation. It is the most important thing a parent needs to know before choosing a university here, so we explain it in plain words.

Confirmed

  • Accreditation is now compulsory: under a presidential decree of 28 August 2025, universities had to pass state accreditation by 1 June 2026. Those that fail cannot admit students or issue state-recognised diplomas.
  • The results are out: on 4 June 2026 the Ministry of Health announced that four universities won six-year accreditation, seven got one year and thirteen failed.
  • Fees are protected: a minimum of US$3,000 a year for foreign medical students, with no increase planned, and the same admission rules at every university.
Worth knowing

The Embassy’s advice — and one open question

On 13 July 2026 the Embassy of India in Bishkek advised students not to enrol at universities that failed accreditation, or at those accredited for only one year. Students at failed universities are being moved to accredited ones; as of September 2026 the Embassy is still awaiting NMC’s word on whether those transfers will count for FMGE.

Our promise: we only recommend universities with full six-year accreditation or state status, and before you pay anything we show you the latest Embassy advisory in writing.

Sources: Embassy of India, Bishkek advisory (29 April 2026); 24.kg, accreditation results (4 June 2026); Embassy advisory (13 July 2026). Rules can change; we re-check them for every student.

Eligibility

  • NEET-UG qualifiedRequired for every Indian student studying medicine abroad. The result stays valid for three years.

FAQs

Questions parents ask us most

Straight answers. Anything else, just ask a counsellor.

Is an MBBS from Kyrgyzstan valid in India?
Yes, if the course meets every NMC FMGL 2021 condition: at least 54 months of study, taught in English, a 12-month internship at the same university, and eligibility for a medical licence in Kyrgyzstan. Since 2026 the university must also hold Kyrgyz state accreditation. You then clear FMGE or NExT to practise in India.
What changed in 2026?
Under a presidential decree of 28 August 2025, every medical university had to pass state accreditation by 1 June 2026. Results announced on 4 June 2026: four universities received six-year accreditation, seven only one year, and thirteen failed. Universities that fail cannot admit students or issue state-recognised diplomas. See the 2026 rules above.
Which universities should I avoid?
On 13 July 2026 the Embassy of India in Bishkek advised students not to enrol at the universities that failed state accreditation, or at those accredited for only one year. Both lists are in the 2026 rules section. Ask us before you pay any university.
How long is the course?
Six years: about five years of study followed by a 12-month internship. Some universities offer five or five-and-a-half-year tracks without an internship there. NMC requires the internship at the same university, so those tracks do not qualify.
How much does a year cost?
An average year costs about ₹6.2 lakh all-in: roughly ₹4 lakh tuition, ₹1.4 lakh hostel and meals, and about ₹80,000 for personal costs, visa and insurance (indicative, Oct 2026). The government minimum tuition for foreign medical students is US$3,000 a year. Six years of tuition typically comes to ₹18–28 lakh. See the fee breakdown.
Is it taught in English?
Programmes for international students are taught in English, alongside Russian and Kyrgyz programmes for local students. Patients speak Kyrgyz or Russian, so you learn basic Russian for the wards. We confirm that your programme is in English throughout, because NMC requires it.
Do I need NEET?
Yes. NMC requires every Indian student who studies medicine abroad to qualify NEET-UG, and the result stays valid for three years. All Kyrgyz medical universities now follow the same admission requirements.
What is the weather like?
Four real seasons. In Bishkek, July afternoons average about 32°C; January nights average about −7°C, with snow. Spring and autumn are mild, with daytime highs around 18°C.
Is Kyrgyzstan safe for Indian students?
Thousands of Indian students live in Bishkek and Osh. During unrest in Bishkek in May 2024 the Embassy of India advised students to stay indoors and kept in touch with them throughout. Register your stay, carry your passport and save the Embassy’s emergency numbers. Our team stays in contact for the whole course.
