Why Kazakhstan

Six reasons Indian students choose Kazakhstan

Not brochure slogans — the things that actually shape your years as a medical student.

Medical schools since 1930

Kazakhstan’s first medical institute opened in Almaty in 1930. Karaganda (1950), Semey (1953) and Astana (1964) followed — state universities with decades of teaching and their own clinics.

A full year on the wards

From the 2026 intake, six years at university are followed by a whole year as an “intern-doctor” — a real clinical role with responsibility, not a few weeks of watching.

Fees in the middle of the range

Tuition at most state medical universities sits around ₹3–5 lakh a year, and living costs are modest. Your family can plan the whole course on one clear budget.

Delhi is a short hop away

Air Astana and IndiGo fly Almaty–Delhi direct in about three and a half hours; Air Astana alone runs 11 flights a week from 30 October 2026. Kazakhstan is just 30 minutes behind India.

Big, green, modern cities

Almaty, home to about 2.35 million people, sits beneath snow-capped peaks; Astana, the capital, is a planned city of bold modern architecture on the open steppe.

A large Indian student family

About 9,500 Indian students were studying in Kazakhstan in 2023, according to the Embassy of India. Seniors from your state, Indian festivals and Indian food are never far away.

The new course · as of Oct 2026

Kazakhstan has moved to a seven-year course — here’s what it means for you

In 2026 Kazakhstan changed how it trains its doctors. If you are joining in 2026–27, this is the most important thing to understand before you choose a university, so we explain it in plain words.

Confirmed

  • Seven years from the 2026 intake: in May 2026 Health Minister Akmaral Alnazarova announced six years at university followed by a one-year internship. The first doctors trained this way graduate in 2033.
  • A real internship year: interns get a new “intern-doctor” status with practical responsibility. Afterwards graduates can work in clinics and hospitals or go on to residency.
  • The Embassy’s advice: on 1 October 2026 the Embassy of India in Astana told students and parents to plan for seven years, not six.
Worth knowing

The “six-year fast-track” — and the licence question

The Embassy says some international students may be able to finish in six years through an accelerated route, under certain conditions. It also says there is no clear official confirmation yet that they will be given a licence to practise in Kazakhstan — and NMC needs that licence eligibility for your degree to count in India.

Our promise: we recommend only the full seven-year route, and before you pay anything we confirm the course length, the same-university internship and licence eligibility in writing.

Sources: Embassy of India, Astana — advisory on course duration (1 Oct 2026); Inbusiness.kz, the Health Minister on the new model (2 May 2026). Rules can change; we re-check them for every student.

Eligibility

Can you apply? The NMC checklist

Every point below must be true for your degree to count in India.

  • NEET-UG qualifiedRequired for every Indian student studying medicine abroad. The result stays valid for three years.
  • 50% in Physics, Chemistry and BiologyIn Class 12, with English as a subject. 40% for SC, ST and OBC candidates.
  • 17 years old by 31 DecemberOf the year you join.
  • Usually no extra entrance testMost universities admit on your Class 12 marks and NEET result; a few hold a short interview.
  • A course that meets FMGL 2021At least 54 months of study, taught in English, with a 12-month internship at the same university — the seven-year course includes one.
  • Eligible for a licence in KazakhstanYour course must let you practise there. That is why we avoid any “fast-track” route until it is officially confirmed.

FAQs

Questions parents ask us most

Straight answers. Anything else, just ask a counsellor.

Is an MBBS from Kazakhstan valid in India?
Yes, if the course meets every NMC FMGL 2021 condition: at least 54 months of study, taught in English, a 12-month internship at the same university, and eligibility for a licence to practise medicine in Kazakhstan. You then clear FMGE or NExT to practise in India. We check each of these for your university before you pay.
How long is the course now?
For students joining from 2026, seven years: six years at university followed by a one-year internship as an intern-doctor, as announced by the Health Minister in May 2026. On 1 October 2026 the Embassy of India in Astana advised students to plan for seven years. Students already enrolled keep their current course. See the 7-year rule above.
What is the “six-year fast-track”?
The Embassy says some international students may be able to finish in six years through an accelerated route, subject to conditions. It also says there is no clear official confirmation yet that they will be given a licence to practise in Kazakhstan — which NMC requires. Until that is confirmed, we only recommend the full seven-year course.
How much does a year cost?
An average year costs about ₹6.3 lakh all-in: roughly ₹4 lakh tuition, ₹1.5 lakh hostel and meals, and about ₹80,000 for personal costs, visa, insurance and registration (indicative, Oct 2026). Six years of tuition typically comes to ₹18–30 lakh; ask each university what the internship year costs. See the fee breakdown.
Is it taught in English?
Programmes for international students are taught in English. Patients speak Kazakh or Russian, and the Embassy of India advises students to be ready to use Russian or Kazakh during the internship year, so start language classes early. We confirm that your programme is in English throughout, because NMC requires it.
Do I need NEET?
Yes. NMC requires every Indian student who studies medicine abroad to qualify NEET-UG, and the result stays valid for three years. Most Kazakh universities admit on your Class 12 marks and NEET result.
How far is Kazakhstan from India?
Chennai to Almaty takes about 9 hours with one stop, usually via Delhi. Air Astana and IndiGo fly direct between Delhi and Almaty in about three and a half hours, and there are direct flights from Mumbai and Goa too. Kazakhstan is 30 minutes behind India.
What is the weather like?
Four real seasons. In Almaty, July afternoons average about 30°C and January nights about −8°C, with snow. Astana is far colder, with January nights averaging about −19°C. Spring and autumn are mild.
Can I work part-time while studying?
No. The Embassy of India in Astana advises that, as of early 2026, foreign students on a student visa cannot work, even part-time, and that working can lead to fines or deportation. Plan your budget for the full course before you go.
