What changed · as of Nov 2026

Kazakhstan has moved to a seven-year medical course

In 2026 Kazakhstan changed how it trains doctors: a full internship year is now a compulsory stage before anyone can practise independently. If you are joining in 2026–27 or later, this is the first thing to understand.

Confirmed

  • Seven years from the 2026 intake: on 2 May 2026 Health Minister Akmaral Alnazarova announced six years at university followed by one year of internship. The first doctors trained this way graduate in 2033.
  • Written into law: amendments to the Code “On the Health of the People and the Health System” make a full internship a compulsory stage before independent practice.
  • Current students keep their course: students already enrolled stay on their original course length, the Minister said.
  • The Embassy’s advice: on 1 October 2026 the Embassy of India in Astana told students and parents to plan for seven years, not six.
Still open

The “six-year fast-track” and the licence question

The Embassy says some international students may be able to finish in six years through an accelerated route, under certain conditions. It also says there is no clear official confirmation yet that they will receive a licence to practise in Kazakhstan — and NMC requires eligibility for that licence before your degree counts in India.

Our advice: treat any “six-year” offer from a Kazakh university with caution until the licence is confirmed in writing. Ask us to check an offer — free.

The new seventh year

What the intern-doctor year really involves

Not a few weeks of watching: a full year of supervised work with patients, under rules set in law.

A full-time clinical post

Interns work full time at a hospital or clinic, with intern-doctor status, their own workplace and access to the medical information systems.

Real work with patients

Interns examine patients, make diagnoses, plan treatment and interpret test results. About 70–80% of the year is clinical work rather than lectures.

Always under a mentor

Each mentor is a doctor with at least five years’ experience, guiding six to eight interns. In serious cases the mentor makes the final decision.

One of six specialties

General practice, therapy, paediatrics, surgery, obstetrics and gynaecology, or paediatric surgery — each intern follows an individual training plan.

Assessed before practice

Mentors assess interns all year. Afterwards, graduates pass a readiness assessment at the National Center for Independent Examination.

Where graduates can work

In Kazakhstan, city polyclinics and district hospitals (first-level care). Larger hospitals still require residency. This matters only if you plan to work there.

NMC check

Does the seven-year course still count in India?

A longer course is not a problem in itself. Here is how the new course measures up against each NMC condition.

  • At least 54 months of studySix years at university is well above NMC’s minimum. FMGL sets a minimum length, not a maximum.
  • A 12-month internship at the same universityThe new intern-doctor year is a full 12 months. Get it in writing that your university runs it for foreign students.
  • Taught in EnglishRequired by NMC for the whole course. The Embassy of India advises students to be ready to use Russian or Kazakh with patients during the internship.
  • Eligible for a licence in KazakhstanAfter the internship and the independent assessment, graduates may practise. Ask the university to confirm in writing that this applies to foreign graduates — and avoid any “fast-track” until its licence is confirmed.
  • NEET-UG qualifiedRequired for every Indian student studying medicine abroad. The result stays valid for three years.
  • FMGE or NExT back in IndiaEvery foreign graduate clears India’s licensing exam before practising here, whatever the course length.

Is it for you?

Who the new course suits — and what to weigh

The seven-year course is not a bad course. It is a longer one, and that changes the maths for many families.

It can suit you if

The extra year is worth it to you

  • You want real responsibility. A full supervised year with patients, before you return home.
  • Your budget covers seven years. Including the living costs of the extra year.
  • You will learn Russian or Kazakh. Patients speak them, and the internship year depends on it.
Weigh carefully

What the change costs you

  • A year later home. You sit FMGE or NExT one year later than on a six-year course.
  • A bigger bill. At least ₹2.3 lakh more, plus any internship-year fee.
  • Details still settling. The six-year fast-track has no confirmed licence yet.

FAQs

Questions parents ask about the change

Straight answers. Anything else, just ask a counsellor.

How long is MBBS in Kazakhstan now?
Seven years for students who join from the 2026 intake: six years at university, then a one-year internship as an intern-doctor. Health Minister Akmaral Alnazarova announced the new model on 2 May 2026, and on 1 October 2026 the Embassy of India in Astana advised students to plan for seven years.
What were the old rules?
Students studied for five years and then completed a combined master’s and internship year — six years in all. Independent practice came only after two to four years of residency, so the full path could take up to ten years. See old vs new.
Does it affect students already studying there?
No. The Health Minister said students already enrolled keep their original course length. Students who joined in 2021 finish in 2027; those who joined from 2022 to 2025 get more practical classes, but the same structure.
Is a seven-year MBBS from Kazakhstan valid in India?
A longer course is not a problem in itself. NMC’s FMGL 2021 rules set a minimum of 54 months of study, English as the medium, a 12-month internship at the same university and eligibility for a licence to practise in the country of study. Get written confirmation from the university that its internship year and licence apply to foreign graduates, then clear FMGE or NExT to practise in India. See the NMC check.
What does an intern-doctor do?
Interns work full time at a hospital or clinic in one of six specialties: general practice, therapy, paediatrics, surgery, obstetrics and gynaecology, or paediatric surgery. They examine patients, make diagnoses, plan treatment and interpret tests, always under a mentor with at least five years’ experience, then pass an independent assessment before practising.
What is the “six-year fast-track”?
The Embassy of India in Astana says some international students may be able to finish in six years through an accelerated route, subject to conditions. It also says there is no clear official confirmation yet that these students will receive a licence to practise in Kazakhstan, which NMC requires.
How much more does the seventh year cost?
At least about ₹2.3 lakh for hostel, meals, personal costs, visa and insurance, plus any fee the university charges for the internship year. An average year in Kazakhstan costs about ₹6.3 lakh all-in (indicative, Oct 2026). See the cost section.
Will I need Russian or Kazakh?
Yes, plan for it. Patients speak Kazakh or Russian, and the Embassy of India advises students to be ready to use Russian or Kazakh during the internship year.
Do I need NEET?
Yes. NMC requires every Indian student who studies medicine abroad to qualify NEET-UG, and the result stays valid for three years.
