Why Nepal

Six reasons Indian families choose Nepal

Not brochure slogans — the things that actually shape your five and a half years as a medical student.

No visa, an open border

Indian citizens need no visa to live and study in Nepal. Fly in, or travel by road to colleges near the border — and come home for any festival.

A home away from home

Dal-bhat on the table, Dashain and Tihar in the calendar, and Nepali written in the same Devanagari script as Hindi. Homesickness has little room here.

English from day one

Lectures, textbooks and exams are in English, with no year lost to learning a new language before you can speak to patients.

Patients like the ones at home

The illnesses you treat on Nepal’s wards are much the same as in India — useful practice for your career back home and for FMGE.

Degrees from established universities

Colleges are affiliated to Kathmandu University or Tribhuvan University, Nepal’s oldest university — each with its own teaching hospital.

A licence on equal terms

Since November 2024, foreign graduates of Nepali colleges can sit the Nepal Medical Council exam and get the same licence as Nepali doctors.

How the course works

From Class 12 to doctor: the Nepal route

Nepal offers a direct MBBS with no pre-medical year. Here is how the five and a half years add up.

  1. 1
    Years 1–2

    Basic sciences

    Anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, pathology and more, often taught organ system by organ system, with early visits to the hospital.

  2. 2
    Years 3–4.5

    Clinical years

    Rotations through medicine, surgery, paediatrics, obstetrics and more in the college’s own teaching hospital.

  3. 3
    12 months

    Internship

    Compulsory, at the teaching hospital affiliated to your college — exactly what NMC asks for.

    Same institution, as NMC requires
  4. 4
    Back in India

    FMGE / NExT

    Clear the licensing exam, complete any internship NMC asks of you, and register to practise.

In all, plan for five and a half years in Nepal, then the licensing exam in India. You can also sit the Nepal Medical Council exam after your internship.

NMC status · as of Oct 2026

What’s confirmed — and what we check for you

In 2024 parents heard that Indian students in Nepal might not get a licence. That question has since been settled. Here is where things stand, in plain words.

Confirmed

  • The licence is on equal terms: on 10 November 2024 the Nepal Medical Council confirmed that foreign graduates of Nepali colleges can sit its licensing exam and receive a licence equal to Nepali citizens — a key FMGL 2021 condition.
  • The course fits the NMC rules: 5.5 years in English, with a one-year internship at the college’s own affiliated teaching hospital.
  • Seats are open: the law lets up to a third of seats go to foreign students. In 2026, 2,428 undergraduate seats were reserved for foreign students and only 476 were filled.
Worth knowing

Seats come through one central system

Every foreign seat is allotted by Nepal’s Medical Education Commission (MEC) through its own portal and merit-based matching. Which college you get depends on your rank and the order of your preferences — so be wary of anyone who “guarantees” a named college outside that process. And like every MBBS abroad, you still clear FMGE or NExT back in India.

How to apply

Two ways into a Nepali medical college

Nepal’s Medical Education Commission (MEC) allots every foreign seat. You pick one of two routes when you apply — here is how to choose.

1
Subcategory I · first priority

Sit Nepal’s MECEE-BL entrance

MEC’s own common entrance exam. Candidates on this route are matched to seats first, on their MECEE-BL merit.

  • Exam200 MCQs · 3 hours
  • Marking+1 right · −0.25 wrong
  • To make the merit list50th percentile
  • Application fee (2026)NPR 8,000
Best for: students who want the widest choice of colleges, and reserved-category students whose NEET percentile is below 50.
or
2
Subcategory II · no extra exam

Apply on your NEET result

Apply without sitting MECEE-BL, using your rank in your home country’s national exam — for Indian students, NEET-UG.

  • Extra examNone
  • NEET needed50th percentile or above
  • SeatsThose left after Route 1
  • Applied onThe same MEC portal
Best for: NEET-qualified students who would rather not sit a second entrance exam.

From the MEC notice for foreign candidates, 2026: applications opened in August, with deadlines extended into September after the Bhotekoshi flood. You choose only one route. Every Indian student still needs a qualifying NEET-UG score for NMC, whichever route you take.

Eligibility

Can you apply? The NMC checklist

Every point below must be true for your degree to count in India.

  • NEET-UG qualifiedRequired for every Indian student studying medicine abroad. The result stays valid for three years.
  • 50% in Physics, Chemistry and BiologyIn Class 12, with English as a subject. Nepal’s MEC also asks for 50% aggregate in PCB.
  • 17 years old by 31 DecemberOf the year you join.
  • MECEE-BL or a NEET percentile of 50+One of the two MEC routes — see how to apply above.
  • A course that meets FMGL 2021At least 54 months of study, taught in English, with a 12-month internship at the same institution — Nepal’s 5.5-year MBBS does this.
  • A seat allotted by MECAdmission to a recognised college comes only through the Medical Education Commission.

FAQs

Questions parents ask us most

Straight answers. Anything else, just ask a counsellor.

Is an MBBS from Nepal valid in India?
Yes, when the course meets every NMC FMGL 2021 condition. Nepal’s MBBS runs 5.5 years in English, including a one-year internship at the college’s own teaching hospital, and since November 2024 foreign graduates of Nepali colleges can sit the Nepal Medical Council licensing exam and receive a licence equal to Nepali citizens. You then clear FMGE or NExT to practise in India. See NMC status.
Do I need a visa?
No. Indian citizens do not need a visa for Nepal, thanks to the open border. You can fly or travel by road. Carry a valid passport, which you also need for your college admission.
Do I need NEET or the MECEE-BL exam?
You need NEET-UG in any case, because NMC requires it of every Indian student studying medicine abroad. Nepal’s MEC then offers two routes: sit its MECEE-BL entrance for first priority on seats, or apply without it if you are at the 50th percentile or above in NEET. See how to apply.
How much does it cost?
Colleges set their own fees for foreign students. As a common reference, Kathmandu Medical College’s 2025–26 fee for Indian students is ₹57 lakh for the full course when the seat is pre-booked. Spread over 5.5 years with hostel, meals and personal costs, an average year comes to about ₹13 lakh all-in (indicative, Oct 2026). See the fee breakdown.
How long is the course?
Five and a half years: four and a half years of study followed by a one-year compulsory internship at the college’s affiliated teaching hospital, all in English.
How are seats allotted?
Centrally, by the Medical Education Commission. You apply on the MEC portal, choose your route, rank your colleges, and MEC matches you to a seat on merit. The law allows up to a third of seats to go to foreign students; in 2026, 2,428 undergraduate seats were reserved for them and only 476 were filled.
What is the weather like?
In the Kathmandu Valley, June highs average about 28°C and January nights about 3°C. The monsoon runs from June to September, with July the wettest month. Spring and autumn are clear and pleasant. Colleges in the southern plains are warmer.
How far is Nepal from India?
There is no direct flight from Chennai; one-stop flights through Delhi or Kolkata take about 7 hours. Several colleges sit near the Indian border and can be reached by road. Nepal is just 15 minutes ahead of Indian time.
