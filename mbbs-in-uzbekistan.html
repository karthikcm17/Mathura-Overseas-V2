Why Uzbekistan

Six reasons Indian students choose Uzbekistan

Not brochure slogans — the things that actually shape your six years as a medical student.

Government universities with history

Samarkand’s medical university dates back to 1930 and Andijan’s to 1955. You study at state institutions with decades of teaching behind them and their own clinics.

Fees within a family budget

Tuition at most government universities sits around ₹3–4.5 lakh a year, and living costs are modest. Your family can plan the full six years with confidence.

Closer than you think

Direct flights from Delhi and Hyderabad, and Tashkent is only 30 minutes behind Indian time. Calls home fit easily into your day, and a trip home is never a long journey.

Cities on the Silk Road

Samarkand and Bukhara are UNESCO World Heritage cities full of blue-domed madrasas and old bazaars. Few medical students get to live somewhere this beautiful.

The homeland of Avicenna

Ibn Sina, whose Canon of Medicine was taught for centuries, was born near Bukhara. Its medical institute carries his name, and you study where modern medicine has deep roots.

Indian food, Indian friends

Established Indian student communities, Indian mess options and Indian grocery stores in Tashkent and Samarkand. You won’t be the first from your town.

How the course works

From Class 12 to doctor: the Uzbek route

Uzbekistan offers a direct six-year MBBS. There is no separate pre-medical degree. Here is how the years add up.

  1. 1
    Years 1–2

    Pre-clinical sciences

    Anatomy, physiology and biochemistry, plus basic Uzbek or Russian classes so you can talk to patients later.

  2. 2
    Years 3–5

    Clinical years

    Ward rotations in the university’s own clinics and affiliated hospitals across medicine, surgery, paediatrics and more.

  3. 3
    Year 6 · 12 months

    Internship

    Supervised clinical practice. NMC requires this year at the same university you studied in.

    We confirm this before you apply
  4. 4
    Back in India

    FMGE / NExT

    Clear the licensing exam, complete any internship NMC asks of you, and register to practise.

In all, plan for six years abroad, then the licensing exam in India. Your counsellor will confirm the exact plan for your university.

NMC status · as of Oct 2026

What’s confirmed — and what we check for you

Parents hear a lot of noise about Uzbekistan. Here is where things actually stand for Indian students, in plain words.

Confirmed

  • Admissions are open: on 28 July 2026 the Embassy of India in Tashkent said NMC’s April advisory is precautionary and not a ban.
  • The rules are the same as everywhere: NMC’s FMGL 2021 regulations apply — at least 54 months of study, English throughout and a 12-month internship at the same university.
  • Ongoing support: the Embassy says it is working with universities on intake norms and clinical training for Indian students.
Worth checking

The three points NMC asked students to verify

NMC’s advisory of 1 April 2026 asked every student to check three things before joining: that the university admits within its approved intake, that you get real hands-on clinical training, and that teaching is in English. These are good questions to ask of any university, in any country.

Our promise: before you pay anything, we confirm all three for your university in writing — plus the 54-month course and the same-university internship.

Eligibility

Can you apply? The NMC checklist

Every point below must be true for your degree to count in India.

  • NEET-UG qualifiedRequired for every Indian student studying medicine abroad. The result stays valid for three years.
  • 50% in Physics, Chemistry and BiologyIn Class 12, with English as a subject. 40% for SC, ST and OBC candidates.
  • 17 years old by 31 DecemberOf the year you join.
  • No extra entrance testUniversities admit on your Class 12 marks and NEET result.
  • A course that meets FMGL 2021At least 54 months of study, taught in English, with a 12-month internship at the same university.
  • A university admitting within its approved intakeOne of the points NMC asked students to verify — we check it for every university.

FAQs

Questions parents ask us most

Straight answers. Anything else, just ask a counsellor.

Is an MBBS from Uzbekistan valid in India?
Yes, if the course meets every NMC FMGL 2021 condition: at least 54 months of study, taught in English, a 12-month internship at the same university, and eligibility for a medical licence in Uzbekistan. You then clear FMGE or NExT to practise in India. We check each of these for your university before you pay.
Has NMC banned MBBS admissions in Uzbekistan?
No. NMC issued a precautionary advisory on 1 April 2026 asking students to check intake capacity, clinical training and the medium of instruction. On 28 July 2026 the Embassy of India in Tashkent clarified that it is not a ban. See NMC status above.
How long is the course?
Six years: pre-clinical years, clinical years in the university’s hospitals and a final year of supervised internship. Make sure the internship is done at the same university, as NMC requires.
Is it taught in English?
International programmes are taught in English. Patients speak Uzbek or Russian, so universities usually add basic language classes to help on the wards. Ask us which programmes are English throughout, because NMC requires it.
How much does a year cost?
An average year costs about ₹6.2 lakh all-in: roughly ₹3.8 lakh tuition, ₹1.7 lakh hostel and meals and about ₹70,000 for personal costs, visa and insurance (indicative, Oct 2026). Tuition for the full six years typically comes to ₹20–25 lakh. See the fee breakdown.
Do I need NEET?
Yes. NMC requires every Indian student who studies medicine abroad to qualify NEET-UG, and the result stays valid for three years. Universities admit on your Class 12 marks and NEET result.
How far is Uzbekistan from India?
Chennai to Tashkent takes about 8 hours with one stop. Centrum Air also flies direct from Delhi (three times a week) and Hyderabad (twice a week) as of 2026. Uzbekistan is only 30 minutes behind India.
What is the weather like?
Four real seasons. Tashkent summers reach about 36°C and are almost rainless; winter days hover around 7°C, nights around freezing, and cold spells can go much lower. Spring and autumn are mild and pleasant.
