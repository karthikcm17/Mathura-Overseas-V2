Why Russia

Six reasons Indian students choose Russia

Not brochure slogans — the things that actually shape your six years as a medical student.

Two centuries of medical teaching

Kazan’s medical school held its first academic council in 1814. Rostov’s traces back to 1915, and Kursk and Volgograd both opened in 1935. You learn where medicine has been taught for generations.

Large teaching hospitals

Universities run their own clinics — Rostov’s has 860 beds and Bashkir’s hospital in Ufa has 755. More patients on the wards means more cases you see with your own eyes.

Fees in the middle of the range

At regional state universities, tuition sits around ₹3–5.3 lakh a year. We point families to these, not to the top-priced Moscow names, so the full six years stay on one clear budget.

Campuses used to the world

About 5,000 of Bashkir’s students come from 64 countries, and more than 30% of Kursk’s are international. Dean’s offices, hostels and teachers here know how to look after foreign students.

A big Indian student family

India’s External Affairs Ministry has put the number of Indian medical students in Russia at more than 15,000. Seniors from your state and Indian festivals are part of campus life.

Cities with a story

Kazan’s white-walled Kremlin is a UNESCO World Heritage Site; Volgograd and Rostov sit on great rivers. Six years here are an education outside the lecture hall too.

NMC rules · as of Oct 2026

In Russia, one question matters most: is it English for all six years?

Russian universities are long-established and well known in India. The risk is rarely the university itself — it is a course that quietly changes language halfway through. Here is what to know, in plain words.

Confirmed

  • The NMC rules (FMGL 2021): at least 54 months of study, taught in English throughout, a 12-month internship at the same university and eligibility for a licence in Russia.
  • Russian is the main teaching language: India’s External Affairs Ministry warns that some “English” courses move to Russian from the fourth year.
  • Back home: every graduate still clears FMGE or NExT and any internship NMC asks for before practising in India.
Worth checking

“Bilingual” courses and the final-year internship

A course that starts in English and finishes in Russian may not meet NMC’s English-throughout rule, and that can cost you your eligibility in India years later. Ask too how the sixth year meets the 12-month internship rule. You will still learn Russian — but for patients, not for your exams.

Our promise: before you pay anything, we get the university to confirm in writing that all six years are taught in English, plus the internship and licence eligibility.

Sources: Ministry of External Affairs — Advisory for Indian students going to Russia; NMC Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate Regulations, 2021. Rules can change; we re-check them for every student.

Eligibility

Can you apply? The NMC checklist

Every point below must be true for your degree to count in India.

  • NEET-UG qualifiedRequired for every Indian student studying medicine abroad. The result stays valid for three years.
  • 50% in Physics, Chemistry and BiologyIn Class 12, with English as a subject. 40% for SC, ST and OBC candidates.
  • 17 years old by 31 DecemberOf the year you join.
  • Usually no extra entrance testMost universities admit on your Class 12 marks and NEET result; a few hold a short test or interview.
  • A course that meets FMGL 2021At least 54 months of study with a 12-month internship at the same university, and eligibility for a licence in Russia.
  • English for all six yearsNot just the first three. That is why we check it in writing for every university.

FAQs

Questions parents ask us most

Straight answers. Anything else, just ask a counsellor.

Is an MBBS from Russia valid in India?
Yes, if the course meets every NMC FMGL 2021 condition: at least 54 months of study, taught in English throughout, a 12-month internship at the same university, and eligibility for a licence to practise medicine in Russia. You then clear FMGE or NExT to practise in India. We check each of these for your university, in writing, before you pay.
Is it taught in English?
Many universities run English-medium programmes for international students, but Russian is the main teaching language in Russia, and India’s External Affairs Ministry has warned that some courses switch to Russian in the later years. NMC requires English for the whole course, so we only recommend programmes that confirm English for all six years in writing. See the English rule above. You still learn Russian to talk to patients.
How long is the course?
Six years of General Medicine: pre-clinical years, clinical years in university hospitals and a final clinical year. Ask exactly how your university meets NMC’s 12-month internship rule — we get the answer in writing for you.
How much does a year cost?
An average year at a regional state medical university costs about ₹6.9 lakh all-in: roughly ₹4.2 lakh tuition, ₹1.8 lakh hostel and food, and about ₹90,000 for personal costs, visa, insurance and registration (indicative, Oct 2026). Six years of tuition typically comes to ₹18–32 lakh. Top Moscow and St Petersburg universities cost more. See the fee breakdown.
Do I need NEET?
Yes. NMC requires every Indian student who studies medicine abroad to qualify NEET-UG, and the result stays valid for three years. Most Russian universities admit on your Class 12 marks and NEET result; a few hold a short entrance test or interview.
How far is Russia from India?
Chennai to Moscow takes about 12.5 hours with one stop, usually via Dubai. There are no non-stop flights from Chennai. Moscow is two and a half hours behind India.
What is the weather like?
Real winters. In Moscow, January days average about −5°C and nights about −10°C, and it usually snows from mid-October to early April. July afternoons average about 24°C. Cities further east, such as Ufa and Orenburg, are colder in winter; the south, around Volgograd and Rostov, is milder.
Can I use my Indian debit or credit card?
No. Visa and Mastercard cards issued outside Russia do not work there as of 2026. Students carry cash, open a local bank account with a Mir card, or use a UnionPay card. Fees are paid in roubles at a bank, against an official university receipt — we walk your family through it.
Is Russia safe for Indian students?
Indian students have studied in Russian cities for decades. Follow the Indian government’s advice: carry your passport, visa and registration at all times, avoid going out alone late at night, take full medical insurance and keep the Embassy of India in Moscow’s contacts saved. Our team stays in contact throughout the course.
