Why Timor-Leste

Six reasons Indian families are looking at Timor-Leste

Asia’s youngest nation, and one of the newest MBBS destinations. Here is what makes it different.

India’s own curriculum, abroad

The MBBS follows Competency Based Medical Education — the framework every Indian medical college uses — taught from Indian and international textbooks. You study what FMGE and NExT will test.

Indian faculty in the classroom

Lecturers from India teach alongside colleagues from Timor-Leste, Cuba, Portugal, Australia and the USA. Familiar teaching, with an international outlook.

One flat, low tuition fee

Tuition is about ₹2.5 lakh a year and stays the same every year, with no donation or capitation fee. It is one of the most affordable MBBS options for Indian families.

A climate just like home

Dili stays between about 21° and 32°C all year. No winter wardrobe, no snow, no shock to the system — and tropical illnesses you will also see in Indian hospitals.

Indian food, safe hostels

An Indian mess in the hostel, 24-hour security, and a separate girls’ block with a resident warden. Parents can rest easy.

A young country close to India

India opened its embassy in Dili in September 2024, and Timor-Leste joined ASEAN as its 11th member in October 2025. Ties between the two countries are growing fast.

NMC status · as of Oct 2026

What’s confirmed — and what a new destination still has to prove

Timor-Leste is new to Indian families, so it deserves straight answers. Here is where things stand, in plain words.

Confirmed

  • Built to the FMGL 2021 length: 54 months of study and a 12-month internship at UCT’s own teaching hospital, taught entirely in English.
  • Recognised at home: UCT is licensed by Timor-Leste’s Ministry of Higher Education and won A-grade accreditation from the national agency, ANAAA, in March 2025.
  • Listed worldwide: UCT appears in the World Directory of Medical Schools, and its graduates are eligible for a medical licence in Timor-Leste.
Worth knowing

A young programme, without a track record yet

The first international MBBS batch began in 2024, so no student has sat FMGE from Timor-Leste yet. NMC also does not publish a list of “approved” foreign universities — it checks each graduate’s course against FMGL 2021. That is why the paperwork for your batch matters.

Is it for you?

Is Timor-Leste the right fit for your child?

Every destination suits some students better than others. Here is our honest view.

A strong fit

Timor-Leste suits you if you…

  • Plan to practise in India. Studying the Indian syllabus from day one is the best start for FMGE and NExT.
  • Need a firm budget. One flat tuition fee, low living costs and no donation make the five and a half years easy to plan.
  • Prefer warm weather and Indian food. No winters to survive, and a mess that cooks the food you grew up on.
  • Study best without distractions. Dili is a small, quiet capital by the sea — not a big, busy city.
Think it through

Weigh it carefully if you…

  • Want a proven FMGE record. The programme is new; the first FMGE results are still a few years away.
  • Want quick trips home. There are no direct flights; the journey takes 20 hours or more each way.
  • Want many seniors from your town. The Indian community is close-knit but much smaller than in Russia or the Philippines.
  • Want a big-city lifestyle. Shopping, nightlife and weekend travel are limited compared with larger capitals.

FAQs

Questions parents ask us most

Straight answers. Anything else, just ask a counsellor.

Is an MBBS from Timor-Leste valid in India?
Yes, if the course meets every NMC FMGL 2021 condition: at least 54 months of study, taught in English, a 12-month internship at the same university, and eligibility for a medical licence in Timor-Leste. UCT’s MBBS is built to these rules: 4.5 years of study plus a 12-month internship at its own teaching hospital. You then clear FMGE or NExT to practise in India.
Is UCT approved by NMC?
NMC does not publish a list of approved foreign universities. Instead, it checks each graduate’s course against the FMGL 2021 regulations. UCT is listed in the World Directory of Medical Schools and holds A-grade accreditation from Timor-Leste’s national agency, ANAAA (March 2025). We confirm every FMGL condition for your batch in writing before you pay. See NMC status above.
How long is the course?
Five and a half years: 4.5 years (54 months) of study followed by a 12-month internship at the university’s own teaching hospital. That is exactly the minimum NMC requires.
Does UCT follow the Indian syllabus?
UCT teaches on the Competency Based Medical Education (CBME) framework that Indian medical colleges use, from Indian and international textbooks, with Indian faculty on its staff. The subjects are the same as an Indian MBBS, which helps when you prepare for FMGE or NExT. See the year-by-year syllabus.
How much does it cost?
An average year costs about ₹4.9 lakh all-in: roughly ₹2.5 lakh tuition, ₹1.75 lakh hostel and meals and about ₹65,000 for personal and local travel costs (indicative, Oct 2026). Tuition is charged for the 4.5 study years and stays the same each year; the whole course typically comes to about ₹24 lakh all-in. See the fee breakdown.
Do I need NEET?
Yes. NMC requires every Indian student who studies medicine abroad to qualify NEET-UG, and the result stays valid for three years. UCT admits Indian students on their Class 12 marks and NEET result, with no IELTS or extra entrance test.
How do I get there from India?
There are no direct flights. From Chennai the journey takes about 20–24 hours, usually through Singapore or Bali, from about ₹35,561 one-way (Sep 2026). Dili is 3 hours 30 minutes ahead of India. We help you plan the route and meet you at Dili airport.
What is the weather like?
Tropical and warm all year, much like South India. Daytime highs stay around 30–32°C. The wet season runs from December to March; June to September is dry, with cooler nights around 21–22°C.
Is Timor-Leste safe for Indian students?
India opened a resident embassy in Dili in September 2024, so help is close at hand. UCT’s hostel has 24-hour security and a separate girls’ block with a resident warden. Register with the Embassy when you arrive, and our team stays in touch for the whole course.
