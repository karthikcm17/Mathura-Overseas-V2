Chapter 01

Your Roadmap After Class 12

An MBBS abroad is not a shortcut — it is a 7-to-8-year path with clear checkpoints. Here is the whole road, so nothing surprises you.

  1. Class 12 with PCB + English INDIAPhysics, Chemistry, Biology/Biotechnology and English, with the minimum qualifying marks. Age 17 by 31 December of the admission year.
  2. Qualify NEET-UG INDIAMandatory if you ever want to practise in India — even with a foreign degree.
  3. Try Indian counselling first INDIAMCC and state rounds. Abroad is a parallel plan, not a replacement.
  4. Choose an NMC-compliant university ABROADVerify course length, English medium, internship and local licence eligibility.
  5. Study 54+ months and a 12-month internship ABROADSame institution, same country, in person. Start FMGE preparation in year one.
  6. Pass FMGE (NExT when it arrives) INDIAThe screening test for foreign medical graduates, held by NBEMS.
  7. 12-month CRMI internship in India INDIAThen permanent registration — you are a doctor in India.
54 moMinimum course length abroad
12 + 12Months of internship: abroad, then India

Is MBBS abroad right for you?

Tick honestly. If most of these are true, you are a good fit:

  • I have qualified (or will sit) NEET-UG.
  • I am ready to live away from home for 6+ years.
  • I will adapt to a new culture and pick up the local language for patients.
  • I will study for FMGE steadily from year one — not in the final months.
  • My family has budgeted the total cost — tuition, hostel, food, travel, insurance.
  • I will choose by compliance and clinical exposure, not by the lowest fee.

Remember the time limit: NMC’s 18 March 2026 notice says the internship must be completed within ten years of joining the foreign institution. Plan for no long breaks.

“Choosing the right university matters far more than choosing the lowest tuition fee.”

Chapter 02

NEET-UG: The Gateway Exam

One exam opens both doors — Indian medical seats and a foreign degree that is valid back home.

NEET-UG is conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA). Qualifying it is mandatory for an Indian student who studies medicine abroad and wants to register to practise in India. A foreign university may admit you without it — but that degree will not lead to an Indian licence.

Eligibility checklist

  • Passed Class 12 with Physics, Chemistry, Biology/Biotechnology and English.
  • Minimum PCB marks as prescribed (commonly 50% General; 40% OBC/SC/ST).
  • 17 years old on or before 31 December of the admission year.
  • NEET-UG qualified at or above the category percentile.

Chapter 03

Counselling — In India & For Abroad

Smart families run two tracks at once: Indian counselling for a government or affordable seat, and an abroad application as a safety net.

How Indian counselling works

MCC — All India counselling

Run by the Medical Counselling Committee. Covers the 15% All India Quota in government colleges, deemed and central universities, AIIMS and JIPMER.

State counselling

Each state fills its own quota (the remaining 85% of government seats, plus private and management seats) on a separate schedule and portal.

Every round: seat matrix published → register & pay fee → fill and lock choices → seat allotment → result → report and join → college verifies your joining.

Chapter 04

The NMC Rulebook: FMGL Regulations, 2021

Notified on 18 November 2021 and still the governing framework in 2026. Get this chapter right and your degree is safe.

54 moMinimum course duration
12 moInternship, same institution
100%English-medium instruction
+12 moCRMI in India after FMGE

The 10 conditions every student must know

  1. 54 months (4.5 years) minimum course, excluding internship.
  2. 12-month internship at the same foreign institution — split internships are not accepted.
  3. English medium throughout; any online study must be compensated by physical attendance.
  4. Curriculum equivalent to Indian MBBS in theory, practicals and clinical training.
  5. Local licence eligibility — you must be able to register as a doctor in that country, at par with its citizens.
  1. Real clinical training with hands-on hospital exposure.
  2. Recognised institution, legally recognised in its own country.
  3. 12 more months of supervised internship (CRMI) in India before permanent registration.
  4. A licensing exam — FMGE today, NExT once it is in force (Chapter 05).
  5. No offshore shortcuts — campuses or “partner” study centres outside the country don’t count.

Always verify on nmc.org.in (Public Notices) and the Indian embassy website of your destination before paying any fee.

Get this chapter right and your degree is safe.

New in 2026 — NMC public notice, 18 March 2026 As of Mar 2026

Students admitted on or after 18 Nov 2021 do one year of CRMI in India after FMGE. Online years not compensated abroad mean 1–2 years of clinical clerkship in India. FMG internship seats: 7.5% of seats in older colleges and 100% in new colleges without their own interns. Internship must finish within 10 years of joining abroad.

Red flags before admission

  • The university can’t show its internship structure in writing.
  • Teaching is “English” only on paper.
  • Classes are partly run from an Indian city.
  • The agent asks for fees in a personal account.
  • Anyone promises “guaranteed registration” or “100% FMGE pass”.

Chapter 05

FMGE Today, NExT Tomorrow

Your degree is only the beginning. The licensing exam decides when you start practising in India.

The Foreign Medical Graduate Examination (FMGE) is conducted by NBEMS for Indian citizens and OCI cardholders with a medical degree from outside India. Pass it, complete your CRMI, and you can register.

300MCQs in two parts
150Marks needed to pass (50%)
0Negative marking
2× / yrJune & December sessions

The pass-rate reality As of Oct 2026

Dec 2025≈ 24%10,264 of 42,872 candidates passed — more than three in four did not.
June 2026≈ 12.4%Result declared 7 July 2026; widely reported as a much tougher paper.
June 2026 first-timers≈ 35%Government data in Parliament: ~88% of candidates were repeaters. Fresh, well-prepared graduates do much better.

Next session: FMGE December 2026 is scheduled for 9 January 2027 (provisional). Pass rates are not officially published by university — treat any “university pass-rate” advert with caution.

What about NExT?

The plan

The National Exit Test is meant to replace both FMGE and NEET-PG with one exam for all graduates — Indian and foreign.

The status As of Oct 2026

Deferred by 3–4 years (NMC Chairman). An NMC draft reported in Aug 2026 keeps FMGE for registration “until NExT comes into force”. Prepare for FMGE.

How to beat the odds

  • Revise from first year — use Indian standard textbooks alongside university notes.
  • Solve MCQs every week from a standard question bank.
  • Treat clinical rotations as exam prep: history-taking, examination, real patients.
  • Join structured FMGE coaching during the clinical years, not after graduation.
  • Take your first attempt seriously — first-timers pass at nearly 3× the overall rate.
  • Stay in a peer study group with other Indian students.

Chapter 06

Myth vs. Fact

Most bad decisions start with a confident sentence from someone who won’t be around when the results arrive.

Myth

MBBS abroad is only for students with low NEET scores.

Fact

Many high scorers choose it for affordability, international exposure or a USMLE pathway.

Myth

Every foreign medical degree is valid in India.

Fact

Only degrees that meet the FMGL Regulations, 2021 — followed by FMGE and CRMI — lead to Indian registration.

Myth

The cheapest university is the smartest choice.

Fact

A ₹2–3 lakh saving means nothing if clinical exposure is weak and you clear FMGE years later.

Myth

English-medium means no local language needed.

Fact

Patients speak the local language. Basic Russian, Uzbek, Vietnamese or Georgian is essential for clinical years.

Myth

“This university has a 100% FMGE pass rate.”

Fact

NBEMS does not publish official university-wise pass rates. Ask for the source of any figure.

Myth

NExT is coming, so I can skip FMGE preparation.

Fact

NExT has been deferred by 3–4 years. FMGE remains the exam for foreign graduates.

Myth

An NMC advisory means the country is banned.

Fact

Advisories are usually precautionary and name specific institutions — e.g. the Indian Embassy in Tashkent clarified in Aug 2026 that Uzbekistan admissions are not banned.

Myth

A “6-year MD” automatically includes the 12-month internship.

Fact

Not always. NMC said in Sept 2026 that this depends on the country’s licensing law — check it case by case (see Georgia).

Myth

Russia is too difficult for Indian students.

Fact

Success depends on individual preparation far more than on the country.

Myth

Final-year coaching is enough for FMGE.

Fact

Continuous preparation from year one is consistently more effective.

Most bad decisions start with a confident sentence. Ask for it in writing.

Chapter 10

Life Abroad & Costly Mistakes

Studying abroad is about adapting and growing up — and about avoiding the errors other families learned the hard way.

Accommodation

Most universities offer on-campus or affiliated hostels — furnished, with Wi-Fi, laundry and 24×7 security. Private flats may be an option after year one.

Food

Popular destinations have Indian restaurants, grocers or hostel messes — especially where the Indian community is large.

SIM & banking

Get a local SIM on arrival, use a forex or international debit card, and track spending in a budgeting app.

Habits of successful students

  • Excellent attendance from day one
  • Learn local-language phrases early
  • Sleep, eat well, stay in touch with family

Safety basics

  • Digital copies of every document
  • Save embassy & university emergency numbers
  • Tell family before travelling to another city

“We don’t just help you secure admission — we help you build your medical career.”

Mathura Overseas helps Indian students pursue quality medical education abroad through honest guidance, transparent counselling and end-to-end support — recommending universities on career goals and compliance, not marketing claims.

Before admission

  • University selection on academics, clinical exposure, faculty and compliance

Visa & beyond
