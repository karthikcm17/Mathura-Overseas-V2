Chapter 01

Why Study MBBS Abroad?

Choosing the right country and university matters more than choosing the lowest tuition fee.

Government MBBS seats are limited and private colleges in India can be expensive. Studying abroad is a practical option — provided the university complies with the NMC FMGL Regulations, 2021.

Why students choose it

  • Affordable — lower cost than many private Indian colleges
  • English-medium programs widely available
  • International exposure to different healthcare systems
  • Clinical experience in affiliated teaching hospitals
  • Modern labs and simulation centres

Is it right for you?

  • You have qualified NEET-UG
  • You are committed to completing the course abroad
  • You can adapt to a new culture and language
  • You will prepare steadily for FMGE/NExT

Chapter 02

NEET & the NMC FMGL Regulations, 2021

Notified on 18 November 2021, these rules set the conditions for Indian students studying medicine abroad to register in India. They remain the governing framework in 2026.

54 mo.Min. course duration
12 mo.Internship, same institution
100%English medium
+12 mo.Internship in India

Step 1 — NEET-UG is mandatory

  • Qualified NEET-UG
  • 10+2 with Physics, Chemistry, Biology and English
  • Minimum qualifying marks met
  • Age 17 by 31 December of the admission year

The 10 rules

  1. Minimum 54 months, excluding internship
  2. 12-month internship at the same institution — no split internships
  3. English medium throughout
  4. Curriculum broadly equal to Indian MBBS
  5. Eligible for local licensure in that country
  6. Adequate hands-on clinical training
  7. Legally recognised institution
  8. Further 12-month internship in India
  9. Licensing exam — FMGE until NExT applies
  10. Don’t trust marketing claims or “100% pass” ads

Red flags: unclear internship structure, curriculum not fully in English, no proof of FMGL compliance, or unrealistic licensing promises. Check nmc.org.in before admission.

Chapter 06 · continued

FMGE, NExT & Your Career After

Your university gives you opportunities. Your success depends on discipline and consistent preparation.
300MCQs, two parts
150/300Passing marks
0Negative marking
2x/yrJune & December

FMGE vs NExT (2026): FMGE remains the only active licensing pathway for foreign graduates. NExT has no confirmed date for this group — don’t defer preparation.

Recent FMGE pass rates have fallen in roughly the 20–30% range. Treat any consultancy’s “university pass rate” claims with caution.

How to improve your chances

  • Start revising from first year
  • Solve MCQs weekly from standard question banks
  • Make the most of clinical rotations
  • Consider structured FMGE coaching in clinical years

Chapter 08

Student Life Abroad

It’s not just about a degree — it’s about adapting and building independence.

  • Accommodation: campus or affiliated hostels with Wi-Fi, laundry and 24x7 security
  • Food: Indian restaurants, groceries or hostel mess in most cities
  • SIM & banking: local SIM on arrival; use a forex card
  • Weather: pack for your destination and season

Safety tips

Chapter 09

Costly Mistakes to Avoid

The biggest regret isn’t studying abroad. It’s choosing the wrong university without research.
  • Choosing a university only because it’s cheap
  • Ignoring the FMGL Regulations
  • Believing “100% FMGE pass” advertisements
  • Choosing a country instead of a university
  • Not asking about hidden costs
  • Waiting until the last minute
  • Ignoring clinical exposure
  • Forgetting FMGE/NExT preparation

Chapter 09 · continued

Pre-Admission Checklist

Before paying any fee, you should answer “yes” to every line.

  • I have qualified NEET-UG
  • I understand the licensing requirements
  • I understand the full course structure
  • I compared multiple universities
  • I know the total cost, not just tuition
  • I spoke with students or alumni
  • I verified the university’s recognition
  • I read the admission letter and invoice
  • I checked internship details
  • My decision rests on verified facts

You’re on the right track if the university shares compliance documents freely, fees are written in full with no “pay today” pressure, and your counsellor compares options with you.
