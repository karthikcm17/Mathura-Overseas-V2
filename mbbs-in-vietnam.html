Why Vietnam

Six reasons Indian students are looking at Vietnam

Not brochure slogans — the things that actually shape your six years as a medical student.

Closer than Europe or Central Asia

Non-stop flights of four to five hours from Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Kochi, and one-stop flights from Chennai. Vietnam is only one and a half hours ahead of India, so evening calls home are easy.

English programmes built for India

Universities such as Dai Nam in Hanoi and Hong Bang in Ho Chi Minh City now run six-year medical programmes taught in English, with intakes made up of NEET-qualified Indian students.

Big-city teaching hospitals

Clinical training takes place in large hospitals in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City — including Cho Ray, one of the country’s major national hospitals — so you see a high volume of patients.

Cases you will see at home

Dengue, tuberculosis and other tropical illnesses are common in both countries, so the patients you treat on Vietnamese wards look a lot like the ones waiting for you in India.

A budget families can plan

Tuition at most universities sits around US$3,000–5,000 a year, and day-to-day living in Vietnamese cities is modest. Your family can plan all six years with confidence.

The Embassy is close at hand

The Embassy of India in Hanoi and the Consulate in Ho Chi Minh City stay in touch with Indian medical students — India’s Ambassador met Dai Nam’s Indian students on Independence Day 2026.

How the course works

From Class 12 to doctor: the Vietnam route

Vietnam offers a direct six-year medical degree, with no separate pre-medical course. Here is how the years usually add up.

  1. 1
    Years 1–2

    Basic medical sciences

    Anatomy, physiology and biochemistry in English, plus Vietnamese classes so you can talk to patients later.

  2. 2
    Years 3–5

    Clinical training

    Rotations through medicine, surgery, paediatrics and more in the university’s partner hospitals.

  3. 3
    Year 6 · 12 months

    Final clinical year

    Supervised practice on the wards. NMC requires a 12-month internship at the same university you studied in.

    We confirm this before you apply
  4. 4
    Back in India

    FMGE / NExT

    Clear the licensing exam, complete any internship NMC asks of you, and register to practise.

In all, plan for six years abroad, then the licensing exam in India. Each university structures its clinical years a little differently — your counsellor confirms the exact plan in writing.

NMC status · as of Oct 2026

What’s confirmed — and what’s still open

Vietnam is a newer destination for Indian medical students, so the honest picture matters more here than anywhere. This is what the Embassy of India says, in plain words.

Confirmed

  • A six-year course with hospital training: the Embassy’s advisory of 5 June 2026 confirms the programme is six years, with clinical practice during the course.
  • English-medium options exist: some universities teach in English. Dai Nam University’s first batch of 208 NEET-qualified Indian students began in October 2025.
  • The same NMC rules apply: FMGL 2021 — at least 54 months of study, English throughout, and a 12-month internship at the same university.
Still open

Vietnamese language and the 2027 licence exam

Patients speak Vietnamese, so the Embassy says students need good Vietnamese for clinical training. To practise in Vietnam, graduates do 12 months of hospital practice and, from 2027, pass a central licensing exam that is likely to be in Vietnamese. It matters because NMC’s rules expect you to be eligible for a licence in the country where you studied.

Where you’ll live

Three very different cities

Vietnam is long and narrow, so your city shapes your weather, your hospitals and your weekends. Here is how the three compare.

The capital · north

Hanoi

A thousand-year-old capital of lakes, tree-lined boulevards and the Old Quarter’s street food. The only one of the three with a real winter.

  • Cool winter (~14–20°C in January); hot, humid summer
  • Home to Dai Nam University
The biggest city · south

Ho Chi Minh City

Vietnam’s busiest, most energetic city and its business centre, with some of the country’s largest hospitals and a lively international crowd.

  • Tropical all year, 31–34°C; rains May–November
  • Home to Hong Bang International University
The coast · centre

Da Nang

A calmer, cleaner seaside city between the mountains and the beach, with the old town of Hoi An a short drive away.

  • Long sandy beaches right in the city
  • Home to Phan Chau Trinh University

FAQs

Questions parents ask us most

Straight answers. Anything else, just ask a counsellor.

Is an MBBS from Vietnam valid in India?
It can be, if the course meets every NMC FMGL 2021 condition: at least 54 months of study, taught in English, a 12-month internship at the same university, and eligibility for a medical licence in Vietnam. The Embassy of India in Hanoi asks every student to check these points with the university before enrolling. You then clear FMGE or NExT to practise in India.
Is it taught in English?
Some Vietnamese universities run English-medium programmes for international students, including Indian students. Patients on the wards speak Vietnamese, so the Embassy of India advises that students also need good Vietnamese for clinical training. Choose a university that teaches Vietnamese as part of the course.
What is the new licensing exam?
According to the Embassy of India in Hanoi (5 June 2026), graduates who want to practise in Vietnam must complete 12 months of hospital practice and, from 2027, pass a central government licensing exam that is likely to be held in Vietnamese. NMC rules expect you to be eligible for a licence in the country where you studied — see NMC status above.
How long is the course?
Six years. Programmes usually begin with about two years of basic medical sciences, followed by clinical training in partner hospitals. Make sure the course includes a 12-month internship at the same university, as NMC requires.
How much does a year cost?
An average year costs about ₹6.1 lakh all-in: roughly ₹3.8 lakh tuition, ₹1.5 lakh hostel and meals, and about ₹80,000 for personal costs, visa and insurance (indicative, Oct 2026). Tuition for the full six years typically comes to ₹18–28 lakh. See the fee breakdown.
Do I need NEET?
Yes. NMC requires every Indian student who studies medicine abroad to qualify NEET-UG, and the result stays valid for three years. Some universities also check your English, through IELTS or an interview.
How far is Vietnam from India?
There is no non-stop flight from Chennai as of 2026; one-stop flights reach Ho Chi Minh City in about 8 hours and Hanoi in about 7. Non-stop flights of around 4–5 hours run from Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Kochi, Delhi, Mumbai and Kolkata. Vietnam is one and a half hours ahead of India.
What is the weather like?
It depends on the city. Ho Chi Minh City is tropical all year, with highs of 31–34°C and a rainy season from May to November. Hanoi has a cool winter, with January days around 14–20°C, and a hot, humid summer of about 33°C.
