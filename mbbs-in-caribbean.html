Why the Caribbean

Six reasons USMLE aspirants look here

This region is not for everyone — but for the right student, these are the things that matter.

Taught the American way

Schools follow a US-style MD: basic sciences first, then clinical clerkships, with teaching built around the USMLE — the exam you need to work as a doctor in the USA.

Clinical years in US hospitals

Many programmes place students in affiliated hospitals in the USA for their clinical rotations, so you learn the American system before you apply for residency there.

Accreditors the US accepts

The region has WFME-recognised accreditors such as CAAM-HP and ACCM — the standard ECFMG has required since 2024 for the USMLE route.

Close to the USA

The islands sit a short flight from Miami, Atlanta and New York, so rotations, interviews and exam centres are far easier to reach than from Asia.

More than one start date

Many US-style schools take students several times a year, often in January, May and September, so you are not tied to a single intake.

Weather like home

Warm and tropical all year — no winters to survive, just a wetter season much like a South Indian monsoon.

How the course works

From Class 12 to the USMLE: the Caribbean route

There is no direct MBBS. You complete a pre-medical course, then a US-style MD in two halves — on the island, then in hospitals. Here is how the years add up.

  1. 1
    About 1–2 years

    Pre-medical course

    Biology, chemistry and the college-level credits a US-style MD expects. Some students with strong Class 12 marks need less.

    Not counted by NMC
  2. 2
    About 5 semesters

    Basic sciences

    Anatomy, physiology, pathology and pharmacology on the island campus, ending with preparation for USMLE Step 1.

  3. 3
    About 5–6 semesters

    Clinical rotations

    Core and elective clerkships in affiliated hospitals — often in the USA, which needs a US visa.

    Check how NMC sees this
  4. 4
    After the MD

    USMLE & the Match

    Pass the USMLE, earn ECFMG certification and apply for a US residency place.

In all, plan for roughly 5.5–6 years after Class 12 before residency. Your counsellor confirms the exact plan for each school.

NMC rules · as of Oct 2026

Built for America — so check twice if India is your plan

Caribbean schools are designed for the US route. That is their strength, and also why they don’t always fit India’s rules. Here is the honest picture.

Confirmed

  • NMC’s rules (FMGL 2021): at least 54 months of study in English, the whole course and a 12-month internship at the same foreign institution, and eligibility for a licence in that country.
  • NMC’s 2025 advisory: students were told not to join three institutions in Belize, listed below, and warned they may become ineligible for registration in India.
  • For the USA: since 2024, ECFMG only certifies graduates of schools whose accreditor is recognised by the WFME.
Worth checking

Rotations abroad and a course under 54 months

Most Caribbean programmes send students to hospitals in another country for their clinical years, and many run about four years. NMC expects the whole course and internship at the same institution, for at least 54 months plus 12. If there is any chance you will want to practise in India, get the school’s answer on each point in writing.

Schools

Accredited Caribbean schools — and how to check any of them

Good and poor schools sit side by side in this region. Start with these six questions, then see the schools whose accreditor is recognised by the WFME.

  • Who accredits you, and is it current?Ask for the accreditor and the expiry date, then check it on the accreditor’s own website.
  • Where exactly are the clinical rotations?Ask for the hospital names and cities, and whether placement is guaranteed.
  • How many months is the MD, and where is the internship?The answer decides whether NMC will accept the degree in India.
  • What are your USMLE pass rates?A confident school shares recent Step 1 and Step 2 results.
  • What is the full cost, including clinical years?Clinical semesters usually cost more than basic sciences — get every term in writing.
  • What is your refund policy?Denied refunds were one of the problems NMC named in its advisory.

Student life

Island life, day to day

Warm weather, English all around and the sea on your doorstep — with a little planning.

Weather varies a little by island. Schools have storm plans for the hurricane season, which peaks around September.

Monthly living cost

Plan roughly ₹70,000–1.4 lakh a month for shared housing, food and transport; US rotation cities sit at the top of the range (Oct 2026).

Food

Rice, beans, chicken and seafood are local staples. Indian groceries are limited on small islands, so most students cook.

Language

English is spoken on most islands and in Guyana. On the Dutch islands, English is widely used alongside Dutch and Papiamento.

Time zone

About 9.5 to 10.5 hours behind India: your morning is your family’s evening.

Staying safe

Live where your school recommends, avoid isolated areas at night, and save your nearest Indian Embassy’s contacts.

Getting there

Usually two or three stops from Chennai, often through the USA. Indian passport holders need a US visa even to transit, so apply early.

FAQs

Questions parents ask us most

Straight answers. Anything else, just ask a counsellor.

Who is this route right for?
Students who are clear that they want to practise in the USA or Canada and plan to take the USMLE. Caribbean schools follow a US-style MD and many place students in US hospitals for clinical rotations. If your main goal is to practise in India, a country whose course is built around the NMC rules is usually a safer choice — compare countries.
Is a Caribbean MD valid in India?
Only if the course meets every NMC FMGL 2021 condition: at least 54 months of study in English, the whole course, training and a 12-month internship at the same foreign institution, and eligibility for a licence in that country. Many US-style programmes send students to US hospitals for rotations and run about four years, so check each point in writing before you pay. See NMC rules above.
Which accreditation matters for the USMLE?
Since 2024 ECFMG only certifies graduates of schools accredited by an agency recognised by the World Federation for Medical Education (WFME). In the Caribbean these include CAAM-HP, ACCM and Grenada’s Medical and Dental Council. Several schools hold conditional accreditation, so check the current status on the accreditor’s own website. See the school list.
Which schools did NMC warn against?
In 2025 NMC advised Indian students not to take admission at three institutions in Belize: Central American Health and Sciences University, Columbus Central University and Washington University of Health and Sciences. It cited poor infrastructure and training, harassment of students, excessive fees and refunds being denied, and warned that students may become ineligible for registration in India.
How long does it take?
After Class 12, students usually complete one to two years of pre-medical study, then an MD of about four years: basic sciences on the island campus, then clinical rotations, often in US hospitals. Plan for roughly 5.5–6 years before the USMLE and residency applications.
How much does a year cost?
At lower-cost schools an average MD year comes to about ₹35 lakh all-in: roughly ₹22 lakh tuition, ₹11 lakh housing, food and local travel, and ₹2 lakh for fees, insurance and visas (indicative, Oct 2026). Basic-science years cost less and US clinical years cost more. MD tuition at these schools typically totals ₹80 lakh to ₹1 crore; the best-known schools cost far more. See the fee breakdown.
Do I need NEET?
NMC requires Indian students who study medicine abroad to qualify NEET-UG if they want to be eligible to practise in India, and the result stays valid for three years. Even if you are aiming for the USA, qualifying NEET keeps the India option open.
What is the weather like?
Tropical, much like South India: warm all year, with a drier season from about December to April and a wetter season from June to November. The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June to November, and schools have storm plans.
Will I need a US visa?
Usually yes. Most routes from India connect through the USA, and Indian passport holders need a US visa even to transit. If your programme includes clinical rotations in US hospitals, you will need a US visa for those years too, so plan for it from the start.
